## Build, one commit per step

1. **Loading, pure part first.** `src/level/models.ts`: `loadModelAsset
   (asset)` parses the data: URL with three's `GLTFLoader.parseAsync` (from
   `three/examples/jsm/loaders/GLTFLoader.js`, bundled by Vite, nothing
   fetched) and caches the scene per asset id; `modelBounds(scene)`. Draco
   and KTX2 are not supported: a GLB that needs them is refused with a
   plain message. Placing clones the cached scene (shared geometry and
   materials). `tint` replaces every material with
   `surfaceMaterial("plain", tint)`.
2. **Game: solid and decor.** A placed model is drawn at pos, rot (degrees
   XYZ), scale. Solid: `box` = its world bounding box as a cuboid collider;
   `hull` (default) = one convex hull of all its vertices after the
   transform; `mesh` = a trimesh of its triangles (static only). Decor: no
   collider. Hidden ones are off until shown (wire show and hide in the
   arena's TriggerHost); register each with the ED-31 move registry if it
   exists by then.
3. **Game: breakable.** With `hitPoints`: add it to the level's
   Breakables (ED-06) as one breakable with that many hit points; at zero
   it is removed and bursts into foam chunks in its tint or its main
   colour. With `voxel`: fill the model's inside with voxel foam instead,
   so it carves like a foam wall: for every 0.1 m voxel centre inside the
   model's bounds, it is inside when a ray from it along +X crosses the
   model's triangles an odd number of times (test against the transformed
   triangles; this is the standard parity test). Those voxels become one
   or more piece volumes in the level's voxel volume, as
   `voxelPrimPieces` does for boxes, each at most 3.2 m a side. The model
   mesh is then not drawn (the voxels are). Cap: a model with more than
   `data/editor.json` `models.maxVoxels` (add it, start 60000) inside
   voxels is refused with a Problems warning and built as hit points.
4. **Editor.** An Import model button (top bar, next to Import) opens a
   file input for `.glb`. Over `models.maxAssetBytes` is refused. The file
   becomes a `ModelAsset` (id from its name, data: URL) in
   `level.modelAssets`, and a Models section in the left tool bar's picker
   lists the level's assets with their names; pick one and click to place
   it (ghost first, sitting on the surface: its bounds' bottom at the hit
   point). Marker: the model itself. Inspector: asset (read only), position,
   rotation, scale (three numbers, plus a lock-proportions tick),
   behaviour, collider, hit points, voxel, tint (palette or none), hidden.
   Deleting the last placed copy of an asset asks nothing; the asset stays
   in the file until "Remove unused models" (a button in the level's
   inspector) drops it.
