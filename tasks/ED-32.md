## Build, one commit per step

1. **Game: `src/level/lights.ts`.** `buildLights(scene, level, quality)`
   makes a `THREE.PointLight` or `THREE.SpotLight` per `LevelLight`:
   colour, intensity (physical units), `distance` = range, `decay` 2, a
   spot's cone from `angle` (full angle, degrees) and `penumbra` from
   `softness`, aimed down its own -Y turned by `rot` (degrees XYZ: put the
   target at pos + the turned -Y). Shadows only when `castShadow`, at most
   `data/editor.json` `lights.maxShadows`, and none on the low quality
   preset. Returns handles by id with `setVisible(on)`, so a trigger's show
   and hide turn a light on and off (wire it in the arena page's
   TriggerHost show and hide). `hidden` starts it off. If `level.lighting`
   is set, it replaces the arena's ambient and sun colour, intensity and
   direction (sunYaw, sunPitch in degrees). Called from `buildArena`.
2. **Editor: placing.** A Light tool in the left tool bar (Things group,
   an inline SVG bulb in `icons.ts`) with a picker for point or spot. Click
   places one a little off the surface (0.5 m along the hit normal), with
   white `#fff6e8`, intensity 20, range 8, spot angle 45 and softness 0.3.
   The editor view shows the real light (so Paul sees its effect while
   building) plus a marker: a small glowing sphere in its colour, and for a
   spot a cone wireframe showing the angle and range. Turn it with the
   rotate handle (spots).
3. **Editor: inspector.** kind, colour (the palette plus a colour input),
   intensity, range, angle and softness (spot), cast shadow (refused with a
   status message over the cap), hidden.
4. **Editor: the level's light.** In the inspector shown when nothing is
   selected (the level's own fields: name, sky), a Lighting section:
   ambient colour and intensity, sun colour, intensity, yaw and pitch, and a
   "Default" button that removes `lighting`. The editor view uses it live.

## Result

Done. The Light tool places point and spot lights off a surface; the game
builds them (colour, intensity in physical units, range, decay 2, spot cone
from the full angle, softness as the penumbra, aimed down -Y turned by the
XYZ rot), shadows at most `data/editor.json` `lights.maxShadows` and none on
the low preset. A level's own lighting (ambient, sun colour/intensity/yaw/
pitch) replaces the arena's defaults. A trigger's show and hide turn a
light on and off (wired in the arena page's own show/hide), and `hidden`
starts it off. The editor view draws the real light, live, plus a marker:
a glowing sphere and, for a spot, a wireframe cone showing the angle and
range; a spot turns with the rotate handle and the [ ] keys. The inspector
sets kind, colour, intensity, range, angle, softness, cast shadow (refused
with a status line over the cap) and hidden, and the level inspector (no
selection) has a Lighting section with a Default button.

Verified: `.swarm/preflight.sh` green (201 files, 1577 passed). Tests
`tests/level/lights.test.ts` and `tests/editor/ed32.test.ts` cover the
spot aim, shadow cap and low preset, show/hide, lighting defaults and the
validator.

Not verified in a browser (no browser here): the Light tool in the left
bar, placing on a surface, the cone and sphere markers, the live view with
and without a level lighting, the shadow cap refusal, and the show/hide
over a trigger. Look at the editor (`npm run dev` or the editor page) and
the playtest from the editor: place a point and a spot light, set
Lighting in the level fields, turn the spot with the E handle and the
brackets, and play the level with a trigger show and hide naming it.

Decision: lighting is edited in the level inspector (the level's own
fields) rather than in an empty inspector, and Default removes the
`lighting` key entirely so the validator and the game fall back to the
arena defaults. A level that has never set lighting shows the arena
defaults as the starting values in the fields, and any edit writes an
explicit `lighting` block.

Small note: `turnSelection` [ and ] turn lights about the vertical of their
own aim (the full XYZ rot is kept, only the Y part moves), the same as a
door's yaw. The editor's real lights use the low preset, so no shadows are
drawn in the view.
