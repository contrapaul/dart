## Build, one commit per step

1. **The textures.** In `src/render/surfaces.ts`, draw each surface once
   into a 256 by 256 canvas (brick, tiles, planks, metal plate with rivets,
   concrete speckle, grass, sand, rock, hazard stripes; plain is no
   texture): light patterns in greys so the thing's colour tints them
   (`material.color` multiplies), seeded with a small fixed PRNG (no
   `Math.random`), `RepeatWrapping`, `SRGBColorSpace`, mipmaps. Keep the
   drawing code in a pure function that writes into an `ImageData`-like
   array, so it is testable headless; the canvas wrapper is tiny. One
   texture per surface, one material per (surface, colour).
2. **World scaled UVs.** `worldUVs(geometry, matrixWorld, scale)` in the
   same file: box-project every triangle on its dominant normal axis in
   world space and divide by the surface's `scale`, so bricks are the same
   size on a small box and a long wall and line up across neighbours.
3. **Game: prims wear their surface.** In `src/level/prims.ts` merge per
   (colour, surface) instead of per colour, with world UVs, drawn with
   `surfaceMaterial`. A prim with no surface looks exactly as before.
4. **Editor: draw and paint.** The editor view draws prims with their
   surface. A Paint tool in the left tool bar (an inline SVG brush): its
   option panel lists the surfaces as small swatches (drawn from the same
   canvases) and the palette colours. Armed, a click on a prim sets its
   surface (and the colour, if a colour is chosen), drag paints every prim
   the cursor crosses, all in one undo step per drag. The prim inspector
   gets a surface select. Physics objects (ED-33) and models (ED-35) use
   `surfaceMaterial` too, so their cards pick this up after merging.
